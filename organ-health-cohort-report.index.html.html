Report 01 / 03 — Cohort Overview

Damage rates barely move — no matter which risk factor you slice by.

Across 2,500 patients screened for organ condition, the share showing damage holds within a narrow 31%–43% band whether you group by smoking status, blood pressure, alcohol use, or family history. The baseline sits at 35.9% — and every segment tested lands close to it.

Report 03 / 03 — Organ Panel

Where damage concentrates, organ by organ.

Liver shows the highest overall damage rate (37.7%) and the widest swing by smoking status — current smokers with liver screens show 42.2% damage vs. 35.8% for never/former.

Damage rate by organ

All five screened organs, sorted highest to lowest

Organ × smoking status matrix

Damage rate (%) — darker cell = higher damage
Standout cell: Liver damage among current smokers sits at 42.2% — the single highest value in the entire matrix, and the closest thing to a real signal in this dataset. Lungs, by contrast, show their highest damage rate among never-smokers (39.6%), running counter to expectation.

Healthy vs. damaged — clinical markers

Average BMI, cholesterol (÷6 for scale) and age by condition outcome
The takeaway: the clinical markers most associated with damage in conventional screening — BMI, cholesterol, age — are essentially identical between the healthy and damaged groups here (cholesterol 207 vs. 209, BMI 29.8 vs. 29.4, age 54.1 vs. 53.9). In this cohort, organ condition doesn't trace cleanly back to any single measured variable.
